World of Warcraft: Forever was announced at the BlizzCon 2026 Opening Ceremony and What's Next panel on Sept 12, 2026, and detailed further at the Deep Dive and Hardcore panels on Sept 13. This page summarizes what has been said since; the [dungeons](/dungeons), [zones](/zones), [editions](/editions), [Skyborne](/skyborne), and [roadmap](/roadmap) pages carry the full detail and sources for each topic, and the [changelog](/changelog) records each fact as it landed.

Two things we published on Sept 12 turned out to be wrong and are corrected below: transmog is opt-out rather than opt-in, and new classes have not been ruled out.

## Setting

- Forever is set after _Warcraft III: Reforged — Forsaken Kingdom_ and before Molten Core, in what Blizzard describes as "year one of vanilla."
- Blizzard calls it "not a mode, a season, or a new version of Classic," and a new permanent pillar alongside modern and Classic WoW.
- The level cap is 60, indefinitely; there are no Outland- or Draenor-style level jumps.
- Inven Global reports from the panel that there is no flying and no level scaling; this is single-source and has not been restated by Blizzard directly.
- The phrase "time bubble" — growth from more raids and dungeons at level 60, not from raising the cap — comes from games.gg's coverage of the panel, not from a Blizzard post, and is treated as single-source.
- Blizzard's stated pillars are approachability, the world as protagonist, the journey, and nurturing social experiences.
- Principal Designer Kris Zierhut, asked why Forever is not a World of Warcraft 2: the engine and server infrastructure have been improved continuously for 25 years, so "why would you throw that away and start over?"

## Rulesets and names

- **Forever has no realms.** Instead of picking from a realm list, players pick one of four rulesets: Normal, where player-versus-player combat requires mutual consent; PvP; Roleplaying; and Hardcore, which arrives after launch.
- Each ruleset is its own large ecosystem, not one giant realm for everyone. You can only group, run dungeons, and raid with players on the same ruleset, and Horde and Alliance still cannot group together.
- Collections and Legacy Points are account-wide across rulesets, with Hardcore as the exception.
- The PvP ruleset will carry faction-balance systems along the lines of Season of Discovery.
- Wowhead reads the absence of a PvP toggle on the Normal ruleset as a soft confirmation that retail's War Mode is not in Forever; that is Wowhead's inference, not a Blizzard statement.
- **Characters have two-part names** — a first and a last name — and the pair is unique per region. Blizzard's example is "Ana Forever." Players also pick a language preference at creation, which the game uses along with faction to lean toward grouping and phasing you with similar players.
- Whether a living character can change ruleset has not been stated. Blizzard has only said that a dead Hardcore character can transfer out.

## Races and class combinations

- **Six new race and class combinations** are confirmed for launch: Gnome Priest, Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock, and Undead Paladin. Blizzard says more are planned.
- Night Elf Paladin belongs to retail Patch 12.2 and is not a Forever combination.
- **Skyborne** is the new playable race, and Blizzard is explicit that it is not a neutral race in the Pandaren sense: you choose Horde or Alliance at creation and the two halves differ. See the [Skyborne page](/skyborne).
- **Every race's racials were reworked** so each has two actives and two passives, tuned for similar offensive power. Racials that used to grant Weapon Skill now grant critical strike chance with the matching weapon type.
- Blizzard's worked examples are Dwarf — Stoneform now reduces physical damage taken rather than raising armour, Find Treasure stacks with other tracking, Mace Specialization grants crit, Big Game Hunter adds 5% damage to Beasts — and Undead, where Will of the Forsaken removes Charm, Fear, and Sleep but no longer grants immunity, Cannibalize restores mana as well as health, and Touch of the Grave is a new drain-on-hit passive.
- Numbers for the other seven races come from outlets that played the BlizzCon demo, not from Blizzard, and Icy Veins and Talents Forever disagree on a few values. The per-race detail is on the [classes page](/classes).

## Classes and talents

- Talent trees keep seven rows per tree and 51 points. The one-point "gold medal" talents at 11, 21, and 31 remain, and **a fourth has been added at 16 points**.
- Talents that existed mainly to unlock a group buff are becoming baseline abilities instead, including Divine Spirit, Blessing of Kings, Improved Mark of the Wild, and Improved Battle Shout. Some talents are unchanged from 1.12.
- Blizzard's stated goal is that every class and specialization is a viable group option, not that they are equal, and that the game becomes "unsolved" again.
- **Paladin is Blizzard's worked example.** Holy Strike returns as a baseline attack at level 6 on a 12-second cooldown, Judgement no longer consumes Seals, the new Seal of Fury is a tanking seal whose Judgement taunts at 10 yards, and Consecration is baseline at 20 with heavier damage on the first four targets. See the [Paladin guide](/guides/paladin).
- Associate Production Director Clayton Stone describes Forever's launch as a deliberately conservative take after Season of Discovery — "we pushed the envelope about as far as we could push it" — and says the team is starting by filling out skills classes did not have. He does not rule out bringing more Season of Discovery ideas in later.
- Ability and talent changes read off the BlizzCon demo are collected per class in the [class guides](/guides).

## Stats and items

- Spell, melee, and ranged **hit chance are now one stat**, and so is critical strike chance. Blizzard says this helps hybrids such as Retribution Paladins and Enhancement Shaman, and makes Taunts, Poisons, and Traps more reliable.
- A new stat reduces the chance for attacks to be parried or dodged.
- Weapon skill works as it did, but items carry less of it each, so it is no longer the automatic best choice.
- Bonus healing now also carries one third as much bonus damage, and appears on gear from level 10. Caster weapons grant spell damage and healing.
- Every dungeon drop has been re-examined, hundreds of new drops added, unique dungeon boss items are now rare quality, and set bonuses improved. Quest rewards were expanded so long questlines stop handing casters two-handed swords.
- Trinkets are being pushed toward situational effects, and some items work only in specific biomes or against specific creature types.
- Combat pacing is unchanged in feel: an ordinary creature takes roughly 10 to 15 seconds to kill solo, and crowd control and threat still matter in dungeons.

## Legacy

- Legacy points come from challenges completed while levelling. **Sixty-five are earnable at launch and each character can spend sixteen.** Points are earned across the account but spent per character.
- The three launch trees are Adventure, Professions, and Resourcefulness, with perks such as Well Rested, Bountiful Harvest, and Reagent Economy, which removes vendor reagent costs from class abilities. More trees are planned.
- A separate Legacy Reward Track grants cosmetics, and mounts are granted when you learn riding.
- The full perk list transcribed from the demo names a crate turn-in currency, Merchant Favor, and two organisations — the Azeroth Commerce Authority and Durotar Supply and Logistics — that Blizzard has not described anywhere. That is single-source and unexplained.

## Camping and professions

- A Basic Campfire creates a campsite with vendors, repairs, profession workspaces, and one-hour buffs. You must sit and become rested before the buffs apply.
- Up to three crafted objects can be added, one per player, sharing a one-hour cooldown. A Blacksmith's Sharpening Wheel raises Attack Power, a Tailor's Faction Banner raises Spirit, a Herbalist's Incense Candle raises Intellect. These do not stack with the equivalent class buffs.
- The first objects are learned at profession skill 20; advanced ones come from Blueprint recipes dropped by dungeon bosses. Cooks can learn upgraded campfires that hold five or ten objects.
- Professions gain over 600 new recipes, and crafted food grants stat bonuses and a small experience bonus.

## Transmog

- **Transmog is opt-out, not opt-in.** This corrects what we published on Sept 12. It is on by default; a mode selector appears at first login, and choosing Classic Mode disables it automatically. You can change the setting later at an NPC.
- With it disabled you see everyone in the gear they actually wear, but you still collect appearances, can still hide your own helm or cloak, and still see other players' hidden helm and cloak settings.
- A bind-on-pickup uncommon or rare dungeon drop grants its appearance to every eligible looter, so nobody rolls against an upgrade just for the look. Epic raid drops grant only to the player the item binds to.
- Armour type rules apply, plate to plate and cloth to cloth, and level restrictions lift at 60.

## Raids, dungeons, and PvP

- Two new raids: Barrow Deeps, a 10-player Night Elf prison cavern with three entrances, and Hyjal Summit, a 20-player raid on Mount Hyjal. Onyxia's Lair remains at 40. New legendary items and tier sets come from both. Raids open Dec 9, 2026, not at launch.
- **The launch raids require no attunement.** Clayton Stone declined to say whether later ones will.
- **There is no flex raiding and none is planned.** Lead Software Engineer Ana Resendes: "We're starting with 10- and 20-man, and no plans for flex at the moment." Difficulty never scales to group size or level.
- **There is no automated group finder.** Blizzard says it will provide tools that help players find each other, but not a button that assembles a group and teleports it into content.
- Nine new dungeons are confirmed by name. See the [dungeons page](/dungeons) for what is known about each, including which level ranges conflict between sources.
- Darkspear Islands is a new 15v15 battleground off the coast of Kalimdor. Original battlegrounds remain. A reworked Honor system and regular season refreshes are confirmed without numbers.

## Hardcore

- Hardcore is confirmed for Forever as a ruleset arriving after launch. The Sept 13 Hardcore panel gave no date beyond the roadmap's "winter."
- A character that dies in Hardcore **can transfer to any other ruleset**, including PvP and Roleplaying, rather than being forced into PvE as in Classic Hardcore. Once out, it can never return.
- A new layer-refresh system warns you before a refresh, lets you get somewhere safe, and then triggers on a button press. One outlet reports this applies to every version of the game, not only Hardcore.
- Legacy runs one way into Hardcore: you earn points there and each death leaves account-wide bonuses, and Hardcore gets its own challenges and perks, but nothing earned outside Hardcore carries in. Everything earned inside carries out.
- Senior Game Designer Josh Greenfield teased a "dedicated space" for Mak'gora and said the team is thinking about Mak'gora tournaments.
- The remaining Hardcore rules are being held for a separate presentation.

## Visuals, controller, and client

- A toggle switches between high-definition and the original low-definition character models, covering your character, NPCs, and other players, with Classic animations restored on the high-definition option.
- Rendering updates add flowing rivers in Elwynn, mist over Darkshore, and moonlight through Ashenvale, while keeping the original art style.
- Official gamepad support is confirmed and was playable at BlizzCon in an alpha state, with abilities on face buttons and the D-pad and a radial menu for map, quest log, and bags. Lead Game Designer Tim Jones on consoles: "I'm sorry. Nothing to announce."

## Beta and dates

- The beta runs Sept 17 to Oct 21, 2026, with a level cap of 30. The roadmap slide shown at BlizzCon reads Oct 22; Blizzard's own post says Oct 21.
- **Beta opt-in is free and open to everyone** on the Forever page on Battle.net. It does not guarantee a seat; the Skyborne Epic Pack and above do.
- Blizzard is hosting a live developer Q&A on Thursday, Sept 17 at 10:00 a.m. PDT on the Warcraft Twitch and YouTube channels, with a VOD afterwards, the same day the beta opens.
- Name reservation runs Oct 27 to Nov 3 for pack owners and covers up to three characters, first come first served.
- Launch is Nov 4, 2026 at 3:00 p.m. PST. See the [roadmap page](/roadmap) for the full table and sources.

## What is not yet known

1. **The addon policy for Forever.** Nothing at all has been said, by Blizzard or in any interview, about addon support, the addon API, combat logging, or whether tools like damage meters and deathlogs will work. This is testable the day the beta opens.
2. Whether a Battle.net API namespace exists for Forever, and what a character profile endpoint looks like for a realmless game.
3. The Hardcore date, its regions, and its remaining rules.
4. Whether a living character can change ruleset.
5. How many Legacy challenges exist, what each is worth, and whether the sixteen-point cap rises.
6. What Merchant Favor, the Azeroth Commerce Authority, and Durotar Supply and Logistics actually are.
7. Dual spec. A locked "Secondary" tab was visible in the demo talent window; Blizzard has not mentioned it.
8. Honor system numbers and whether Darkspear Islands has ranked play.
9. Whether Classic Era characters can transfer into Forever.
10. The identity of the "notorious Demon Hunter" teased for Barrow Deeps, and whether a raid referred to as "Timbermaw" in a Wowhead navigation title is real.
11. Full talent rank scaling. All three public calculators warn their rank values are estimated.
12. Level ranges and quest counts for Mount Hyjal and Shen'dralar.
13. Whether post-launch raids will have attunements.
14. Pricing verbatim from Blizzard's own copy.
