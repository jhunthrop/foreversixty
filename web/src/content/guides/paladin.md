Paladin is the class Blizzard chose to show off the Forever class rework in full, so more is known about it than about any other class.

## New race pair

**Undead Paladin** is confirmed, first through an announcement cinematic and then in Blizzard's own list of six new combinations. Night Elf Paladin belongs to retail Patch 12.2 and is not a Forever combination.

## Talent structure

Talent trees keep the familiar shape: seven rows per tree and 51 points to spend. The one-point "gold medal" talents at 11, 21, and 31 points are still there, and Forever adds a fourth at the 16-point mark, so every tree has four of them. Principal Designer Kris Zierhut confirmed the 16-point addition in an interview.

Talents that existed mainly to unlock a group buff are becoming baseline abilities instead, which frees room in the trees for talents you notice. Some talents are unchanged from 1.12.

Blizzard counted 20 new, 18 changed, and 14 unchanged talents across the three Paladin trees, per one outlet's tally from the demo. That is the most new talents of any class except Warlock.

## What changed in the demo

**Baseline abilities**, all confirmed by Blizzard:

- **Holy Strike** returns as a baseline attack at level 6: instant, 12-second cooldown, weapon damage plus Holy damage. It had reached rank 5 by level 38 in the demo. Talents in all three trees build on it.
- **Judgement no longer consumes your Seal**, which removes the constant re-casting. It has a 10-yard range and a 10-second cooldown.
- **Seal of Fury** is a new tanking seal. Every hit adds Holy damage regardless of weapon speed, and with a shield equipped you get an absorb worth about half of it. Judging with it taunts, at 10 yards where every other tank's taunt is 5. It had reached rank 4 by level 38.
- **Consecration is baseline at level 20**, dealing light damage to everything inside it but much heavier damage to the first four targets, so Paladins cannot trivially pull a whole room.
- **Blessing of Kings** is trainable rather than a talent.

**Holy talents:** Improved Holy Strike reduces its cooldown, Voice of Truth grants a silence and interrupt immunity window, Reverence and mana regeneration while casting make Spirit worth gearing for, Divine Favor has moved up to make room, Infusion of Light is new, Holy Shock is easier to reach with a shorter cooldown, Consecrated Ground supports a damage-dealing Holy build, and Light's Vigil marks a target so the next Holy Shock has no cooldown and either heals the party or adds damage.

**Protection talents:** Improved Seal of Fury and Shield Specialization help mana economy, with blocks able to restore mana; Swift Judgement resets Judgement when it misses; Templar's Bulwark grants a large absorb in exchange for Forbearance; Reckoning is improved; and Iron Creed is new, letting Holy Strike passively reduce incoming damage.

**Retribution talents:** Vindication now works like Demoralizing Shout or Roar; Sacred Arbiter keeps Judgements refreshing; Champion of the Light gives a reason to gear Intellect; Instrument of Law makes Hammer of Wrath instant; and Twist of Light echoes your previous Seal when you swap, giving the benefit of two at once — seal twisting without a swing-timer addon. Blizzard notes that Ret players who dislike seal twisting can skip it and take Holy Shock from the Holy tree instead.

**Other changes**, from one outlet's reading of the trees: Illumination refunds roughly 50% of the mana on a crit heal rather than the full cost, Redoubt triggers on any damaging melee hit instead of requiring a crit, Holy Shield's block chance bonus is lower, and Templar's Bulwark's absorb is equal to 100% of max health.

Blizzard is keeping Paladin weaknesses in place deliberately: no standard interrupt, and limited tools for slowing enemies at range.

**A naming note:** one outlet calls the tanking seal "Seal of Wrath." Blizzard's own recap, Wowhead, and the demo spellbooks all call it Seal of Fury.

## Where these come from, and what they are worth

Except where a line is credited to Blizzard, everything in the section above was read off BlizzCon demo footage and show-floor screenshots by fan sites, mostly at level 38, which was the demo cap. Numbers can be wrong, ranks can be misread, and anything can change before launch. Nothing here is datamined.

Associate Production Director Clayton Stone has also said that Forever's launch is a deliberately conservative take after Season of Discovery, and that the team is starting by filling out skills classes did not have rather than reinventing them.
