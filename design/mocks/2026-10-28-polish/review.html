1. Two directions — pick one

Both are modern and gaming and both drop Cinzel from headings (it stays in the wordmark only). A · Frost is cooler and sharper (Rajdhani headings, Inter body, steel surfaces, tight radii, sentence-case buttons). B · Forge is warmer and heavier (Barlow Condensed 800 headings over today's Barlow body, gold-edged surfaces with glow, rounder radii, uppercase buttons). The designer recommends B: it keeps the body face and every tuned line length, adds about 45 KB of fonts, and is the raid-tool and launcher voice.

2. Header: the account control

The character selector becomes the account control: same closed face (crest, name, spec and level), its panel gains an account head on top and Account, your characters, your plan and Sign out at the bottom. The community Discord link leaves the header for the footer and the Guild menu. Signed out, the right end is Sign in with Battle.net.

3. Footer

A brand block (seal, name, the one-line promise, Join the Discord) beside four labelled link columns; the trademark and operator lines small and last.

4. Components: before and after

One component set replaces the plain rows, native checkboxes, bare underlined links and outline-only buttons: settings rows with an icon slot, switches, button recipes, tags, icon and stat tiles, selects, action links. Applied first to the account page's Plan and Discord blocks.

5. /premium without the example card

The empty example card is gone. Column 3 becomes a static sample coach review rendered at build time from a real fixture, labelled Sample in its first line and caption (the coach is live and is the first Premium benefit). The copy of the example on the /sim landing is deleted, not replaced. The boards in §1 show the row.

Tokens and the heading face land first (one commit, both directions are the same components), then the header and footer, then the account blocks, then the /premium row, then the inventory in rank order.
