# Healer kit (2026-10-08)

Three gaps in what a healer can use, closed from the client's own rows, and what they did to the ranker's healer
numbers. The rule the healer lane keeps still holds: **a healing sim ranks gear under a stated incoming-damage
profile. It never says one healer class beats another, and every published healer number carries the profile's
name.** The tables below put each spec next to its own earlier run; nothing compares specs.

## 1. Cooldowns that only change the caster

The major-cooldown pass (fork `shouldActivateHelper`) refused every cooldown that was not flagged helpful when the
current target was a friend, which is a healer's tank. The earlier fix (potions, runes, Inner Focus) flagged those
spells helpful one by one. Racials and stat trinkets stayed out.

The gate is now about the cooldown's own kind. `MajorCooldown.SelfBuff` says the cooldown only changes the caster, so
it does not care whom the caster targets:

| Cooldown | Kind | With a friendly target |
|---|---|---|
| Berserking, Blood Fury, Eureka!, Stoneform, Elune's Light | racial, self buff | used |
| Stat trinket with a caster stat (spell power, healing, spirit, intellect, mp5, haste, school power) | self buff | used |
| Stat trinket with only attack power, armor penetration and the like | offensive | stays out |
| Smolderweb's Eye (a damage over time on its target) | offensive | stays out |
| Touch of the Grave | a proc, never a major cooldown | unchanged |

The kind of a trinket is read from the stats it grants (`casterStats` in `sim/core/major_cooldown.go`), not from a
list of item ids, so a new use trinket is classified when it is registered.

Tests (`sim/priest/healing/cooldowns_test.go`, a troll Holy priest against the fake raid): Berserking fires, a
Talisman of Ephemeral Power (spell power) fires, Earthstrike (attack power only) never does. Both failed with the
`SelfBuff` clause removed.

## 2. Forever's healer consumables

Read from the client's item, spell and aura rows (build 1.60.1.70009):

| Item | Id (spell) | Effect | Engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greater Mageblood Elixir | 250341 (1250948) | aura 85, 20 mana per five seconds, 30 min, level 55 | `ManaRegenElixir.GreaterMagebloodElixir` |
| Cleric's Elixir | 250332 (1250925) | aura 135, 30 healing, level 45 | `HealingPowerBuff.ClericsElixir` |
| Greater Cleric's Elixir | 250333 (1250926) | aura 135, 40 healing, level 55 | `HealingPowerBuff.GreaterClericsElixir` |
| Elixir of Sages | 250338 (1250941) | aura 29 spirit 25, aura 290 spell critical 2 percent, level 55 | `SpiritElixir.ElixirOfSages` |
| Sage's Tea | 249870 (1249925, well fed 1249927) | 44 healing for the well-fed quarter hour, level 45 | `Food.FoodSagesTea` |

The raid preset's healer group now carries Greater Mageblood (in place of the plain Mageblood Elixir, which stands
below it), Greater Cleric's Elixir, Elixir of Sages and Sage's Tea, each with its reason. `IDS.md` was regenerated by
`go run ./internal/genids`, and `builds/1.60.1.70009/simbuffs.json` names the five new ids through the ordinary name
join (no curated override was needed); the data test that every id is named passes.

Left out, with the reason:

- Elixir of Wicked Regeneration (mana 10 per five seconds, 9 health): the same slot as Greater Mageblood and
  strictly less mana.
- Elixir of Spirit, Greater Spirit and Intellect (10, 18, 10): not mana or healing consumables; Elixir of Sages
  covers spirit at the same level.
- Major Rejuvenation Potion (level 50): one potion cooldown, shared with the mana potion the lists already drink.
- Smoked Desert Dumplings, the Zanza line and anything from Ahn'Qiraj or Naxxramas.

**Assumptions to flag.** (1) The client tables carry no battle or guardian slot for elixirs: every elixir shares
spell category 79 and a three second category cooldown, nothing more. The preset therefore stacks three elixirs and a
flask, as the existing preset already stacked a flask and Mageblood. If Forever limits elixirs, the healer group is
too generous by one or two entries. (2) Where the Forever-new recipes come from is not in any table the site reads
(classic-db carries none of them). Nothing names Ahn'Qiraj or Naxxramas, the Greater tier is level 55 and the
recipes are BoP, and the alchemy skill ranks that make them trivial are 180 to 285, so they are treated as
available at launch. A source for each is a follow-up. (3) The client has no icon for the five new ids, so
`simbuffs.json` carries the build's placeholder for four of them (as it already does for the Greater protection
potions), and Sage's Tea shows the Sagefish Delight icon.

## 3. Shadowfiend and Dark Sacrifice

Both are Shadow Magic abilities learned at the levels below (SkillLineAbility acquire method 0, so trainable).

| | Client rows | Engine |
|---|---|---|
| Shadowfiend 401977 | level 1, no cost, 1.5 s global, 5 min cooldown, 15 s summon; Mana Leech 401988 (energize percent, 5) "when the Shadowfiend attacks" | self hot of 15 s; 5 percent of maximum mana per attack |
| Dark Sacrifice 1277324 to 1277328 | levels 20, 30, 40, 50, 60; 1.5 s global, 10 min cooldown; 15 s aura, every 3 s takes 80 / 136 / 196 / 258 / 320 health (aura 226) and gives the same mana (aura 24); text: "gain ${$o2+$SPI} Mana" | five ticks; each takes the rank's health and gives that much mana plus a fifth of the caster's Spirit |

**The pet's swing timer is not in the client tables.** The summon effect names creature 202079 and properties 5590;
no table the site reads has the creature's attack speed. The engine does not summon a pet. It returns the Mana
Leech on a swing of 1.5 seconds over the client's 15 second summon: ten returns of 5 percent, every swing landing, no
misses, no damage from the fiend. **The 1.5 second swing is an assumption**; the 15 seconds and the 5 percent are the
client's. The Spirit term in Dark Sacrifice is the spell text read literally, spread evenly over the five ticks.

Both are in the Holy and Discipline lists behind a mana gate: Shadowfiend below half the bar, Dark Sacrifice below
70 percent with the healer's own health above 70 percent. They sit directly after the autocast line. The conformance
golden carries the new spells; Shadowfiend matches the client except that the engine's required level is now the
client's 1.

### Sensitivity to the swing

The same priest runs at a 3 second swing (half the returns), raid preset, band 60, effective HPS and the mana
lasting figure:

| | 1.5 s swing | 3 s swing |
|---|---|---|
| Holy priest, alliance | 514 HPS, 2,290 s | 514 HPS, 930 s |
| Holy priest, horde | 514 HPS, 2,053 s | 514 HPS, 729 s |
| Discipline priest, alliance | 511 HPS, 2,206 s | 510 HPS, 817 s |
| Discipline priest, horde | 511 HPS, 2,216 s | 510 HPS, 758 s |

Healing per second hardly moves; the mana-lasting figure moves a lot (and is a simulated extrapolation past the 300
second fight, so read it as "the bar does not run dry", not as a duration). Whatever the swing, both priests now last
the fight under the profile.

What the table can and cannot say:

- Every spec's mana now lasts the 300 second fight on both factions; the horde Holy paladin, the narrowest before
  (299.7 s), has room. The priests' figure rises most because they have two new mana spells besides the elixirs.
- Effective HPS rose for the priests and the paladin and stayed within two percent for the shaman and druid. Overheal
  rose a little where the healer now has mana to cast more and hurt less. The shaman's HPS did not rise because its
  mana was not what bound it.

- The split between the three changes (self-buff cooldowns, elixirs and tea, the two priest spells) is not measured.
  The druid and the shaman show what the first two do without the third; the priests' extra is the third.
- No row says one healer is better than another. The profile, rotation and gear differ by spec.
