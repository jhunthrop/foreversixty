### 1.3 What the audit says

1. The addon has four chromes (Panel and tour in the site style, the Builds dialog and Settings flat, the chip its own, the guild and group flyouts a fourth) and three doors that open different windows for the same click.
2. Every fact the player needs is on screen two to five times in different words: the next talent (5), upgrades (3), tonight's raid (4), builds waiting (4), the source filter (2), the spec choice (4).
3. Nothing has one home. The fix is a home for every fact, one door to it, and marks in the world that point there.

## 2. The model

- **One window, the Forever Sixty window.** Every fact the addon owns has one tab. The window is the only addon frame with chrome; it opens on the tab that matters right now.
- **Marks stay where the game puts the thing.** Tooltips, slot marks, bag arrows, talent marks and glow, reward marks and chat badges stay in the world, say one thing, and a click (where the client allows one) opens the tab that owns the detail.
- **One launcher.** The chip beside the minimap: the crest and one line, the most urgent thing; a click opens the window on that thing's tab. The addon compartment entry and the optional minimap button do exactly the same; the doors on the character pane and the guild frame open their tab.

## 3. The frame, shared by every tab

The builds-window lane builds the frame (`surfaces/Hub.lua`); this section fixes what every later tab must keep.

### 3.1 Sizes

740 wide (`Hub.width()`: padding 16 + `windowLeft` 388 + `windowGutter` 32 + `windowRight` 288). Height fits the active tab's taller column (`Hub.heightFor`), never under `Theme.SIZES.dialogHeight`, never scrolls: a tab that would outgrow the screen caps its list and says how many are hidden (the Officer roster's `16 of 31 shown`). Header 56, tab strip 28, body padding 12, footer 28. One height per open window: the tallest available tab's, capped to the screen, so switching tabs never moves the footer; a shorter tab ends in ground. (The boards are cropped to each tab's content; the built window is not.) Pixel-snapped through the shared dialog factory. Draggable by the header; position remembered; `Reset positions` centres it. Strata DIALOG. Closes on combat start and does not reopen by itself; Escape closes it (after the active tab has used Escape for its own step back).

### 3.2 Header (56)

- The ringed HD class crest, 32 px (`Theme.buildCrest`, never a square class icon).
- Title: the character's name, title face 16 px, cream. The same on every tab. (The builds-window lane's `Hunter builds` becomes the name when the second tab lands; the class sits in the line under it.)
- Figures line, muted 12 px: `Level 60 Hunter` first on every tab, then at most two figures the active tab owns: Me `Thornwake Company · Beast Mastery raid build`; Builds `27 of 33 on plan · 5 off plan` (the warning colour, hover names the talents; with nothing off plan, the points waiting in the client gold, `1 to spend`); Gear `9 of 16 best in slot · 2 enchants missing`; Rotation `Beast Mastery raid build · followed 85% on Tuesday`; Guild `Thornwake Company · Officer` (the player's guild rank name); Officer the same, or `Master looter` while a session runs.
- The close X, 20 px, at the right. A 1 px `line` rule under the band.

### 3.3 Tab strip (28)

`Widgets.tab` for every tab: label in the body face, muted; the active tab gold with a 2 px gold underline; hover lifts the label to the body colour; keyboard focus a 1 px gold ring. Minimum width 96 (`Theme.SIZES.tabWidth`), 12 px label padding, 4 px apart, left-aligned from the padding. A tab carries a 6 px client-gold dot after its label exactly while `Hub.urgent()` (3.6) has an item for that tab: Guild while tonight's raid is not signed up or a job is new, Builds while a point waits or builds arrived from the site, Gear while a bag item beats what you wear, Me after a raid night until it is seen, Officer while a master loot session is open. The strip shows from the moment two tabs exist (it hides only while the builds-window lane ships Builds alone).

Order and availability (`Hub.register` `order`):

| Order | Tab | Shown when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Me | always |
| 2 | Builds | always |
| 3 | Gear | level 10 or more (the first band, `Gear.bandFor`) |
| 4 | Rotation | the spec has a rotation at the character's level (`Data.rotations`) |
| 5 | Guild | in a guild |
| 6 | Officer | in a guild and `C_GuildInfo.CanEditOfficerNote()` (the client's `CanEditOfficerNote` where that is the API) answers true; also, with only its Master loot section, while you are master looter in a session |

The officer gate replaces `Comms.canPush`'s rank index check everywhere (build push, roster snapshot asks, approvals). A guild that gives a rank officer-note rights trusts it with the roster; one gate, the client's own.

No key switches tabs: the keys belong to the action bars, and the window passes every key it does not use through (as the Builds window does). Tabs are clicked; `/fs <tab>` opens one.

### 3.4 Footer (28)

A 1 px rule above. Muted at 75 % (`Hub.HINT_ALPHA`). Left: `Esc to close`, or the active tab's step back (`Esc for your builds`, `Esc for the roster`). Right: what a click does now on that tab, or on Me the companion's freshness (`Companion synced 14 min ago`, `No companion`, `No ratings pack`, `Ratings 8 days old` in the warning colour). The score's own date stays in its section heading (`as of 9 Dec`), because the date changes what the number means; nothing else repeats it.

### 3.5 Type, colour, controls

One vocabulary, the web site's, through `Tokens.lua`:

- Text 12 px Open Sans on 15 px lines (`Theme.SIZES.dialogText`); section headings in the title face, cream, 14 px (the Builds tab's `BuildsWindow.buildSection`, as the planner's panel titles: brand review 2026-11-13, carried by every tab), 18 px rows, a muted right-hand note cut to fit; the one big figure per tab (the score) 24 px semibold.
- Colours: body, muted, strong, cream (title), gold (the active tab and a link), the client gold `#ffd100` for something the player must act on now (`Sign up`, `Not signed up`, `In your bags`, the job, `Spend 1 point`), success green for a gain or a yes, warning for a loss, a miss, `Out`, `No enchant`. `Best in slot` on the doll is muted: it is the quiet state. Item names in their quality colours, player names in their class colours, percentiles in the parse colours, the score in its tier colour. Nothing else gets a colour.
- Buttons: `Widgets.clientButton` only: blue with a white label for an action or an unchosen option, teal for the chosen one of a set (the sign-up answer, the level, the Conditions, an armed `Give`). Text links in gold for going somewhere (`Guild`, `Show all`, `Default`, `Full log`, `Roster`). No other button recipe.
- Sections are a heading and rows; no boxes, no cards, no edge bars, no rules inside a column. The one vertical rule runs between the columns. A table never crosses it: every table has fixed column widths that sum to its column, and a cell that would overflow is cut with an ellipsis and shown whole on hover.
- Every item shows its icon with the quality edge and the client's own item tooltip on hover; shift-click links it. Every spell shows its icon and the client's spell tooltip. Every player name is class-coloured; click whispers, shift-click invites, right-click opens the client's unit menu.
- Copy: the web's words where the web has the thing (`In`, `Late`, `Bench`, `Out`, `Not signed up`, `as DPS`, `Tanks`, `Healers`, `Melee`, `Ranged` from `web/src/lib/guild/raid-copy.ts`; `316 to Epic (1500)`, `410 of 500` from `web/src/lib/score/copy.ts`; `Conditions`, `Raid-ready`, `Bare` and the source kinds from `web/src/lib/bis/copy.ts` and `source-filter.ts`). Plain numbers, no thousands separator, as the web.

### 3.6 Which tab an open lands on

`Hub.urgent()` keeps an ordered list of items, each with its tab: tonight's raid not signed up or a new job (Guild), a master loot session (Officer), no spec chosen, a point to spend or builds from the site (Builds), a bag item that beats what you wear (Gear), a raid night not yet seen (Me). `/fs` and the key binding (an open with no tab asked) land on the first unseen item's tab; opening that tab marks the item seen, and the next open lands on the next unseen item, else on the tab you last had. An item comes back only when its facts change (a new raid, a new item, a new point). The chip is different on purpose: its line is the first item whether seen or not, and a click on it always opens the tab that line names, so the chip never promises one thing and opens another.
