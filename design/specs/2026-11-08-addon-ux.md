## Copy rule (owner, 2026-11-08)

"Adding unnecessary explanations is a dead giveaway it's AI." A number gets its unit and nothing else; any qualifier the context already makes clear is cut. On the tooltips: the green arrow and the figure are the upgrade (`+3.5 DPS`, never `Upgrade +3.5 DPS`); a loss is its figure (`−1.3 DPS`); `Best in slot`, never `Your best in slot`; a level refusal is its reason (`Mail armor at level 40`); `over <worn>` only on rings and trinkets. Where a table below still shows an older wording, this section wins. Every surface passes ai-slop-reviewer alongside ux-designer and wow-player before it lands.

## 2. Tooltip redesign

### 2.1 The grammar, one for every tooltip

- **G1. One answer, then at most one supporting line.** No header, no blank row. Our lines follow the client's last line directly, like its own coloured lines. Everything else is behind Shift.
- **G2. Colour is meaning**, in the client's own font colours:
  - green `GREEN_FONT_COLOR` (1aff1a): do this (an upgrade, in your bags);
  - gold `NORMAL_FONT_COLOR` (ffd100): yours (your best in slot, your job tonight);
  - white `HIGHLIGHT_FONT_COLOR` (ffffff): information (next upgrade, no upgrade until);
  - grey `Theme.tooltipMuted` (b8b8b8, lighter than the client's 808080 for contrast on the tooltip ground): no, or detail;
  - item names always in their quality colour; gains always green.
- **G3. The green arrow means upgrade everywhere**: the client's `bags-greenarrow` atlas at 12 px (`|A:bags-greenarrow:12:12|a`) leads every green upgrade line, as it marks the bag item, the paper-doll slot, the quest reward and the roll frame.
- **G4. Shift is details**, the same key the client uses to compare. No tooltip ever prints a "hold Shift" hint; the welcome teaches it once.
- **G5. Silence over noise.** No data, not gear, not loaded yet: no line at all. Never `…`, never a zero.

### 2.2 Item verdicts, exact text

Precedence top to bottom; the first that applies is the verdict. `N` is the next band's level; gains are `Progress.roundedGain` with the existing 1.0 floor (`Progress.MIN_GAIN`), one decimal for DPS and HPS, none for score.

### 2.3 The Shift block, exact order

Appended under the support line, all grey unless named:

1. The figure's breakdown, every stat that moves including Stamina and Armor: `+7 Agility, +2 Stamina, +6 Armor` in the client's own stat words (no brackets); under a worn item's next upgrade it names that item, `Ranger Bow: +1 Agility`. A weapon's own DPS is never listed (owner, 2026-11-08): `+3.4 weapon DPS` under a `+3.5 DPS` headline read as one number twice. Armor and Stamina are listed even though the weights price them at nothing for a damage spec, so a leveller sees what the swap costs in survival.
2. Alternatives, at most two, one per line: `Also good: <item> +2.2 DPS (gun)` (an alternative under the 1.0 floor is left out), the second aligned under the first item name; a row never crosses the tooltip's border (it shortens the name to the client's own widest line). A ranged item whose type differs from the worn one carries its type in the game's words, `(gun)`, `(bow)`, `(crossbow)`, `(thrown)`, `(wand)`, on alternatives and on the Next line alike; `(thrown)` marks a thrown weapon in the ranged slot (item subclass 16, e.g. Cracked Blacksmith Hammer, inventory type 25 in the 1.60 data).
3. (Cut by the copy rule: the attribution already says whose numbers these are.)
4. A cap: `Hit capped: more is wasted`.
5. `Enchant not counted` when the worn enchant could not be read.
6. Last, dimmer (8a8a8a): `Forever Sixty · Beast Mastery` or `Forever Sixty · Beast Mastery, your sim`.

Holding or releasing Shift redraws the tooltip through the client's own `SetHyperlink` from an event frame (the mechanism `PlayerTooltip.lua` already uses). No `OnUpdate`.

## 3. First-run onboarding and the chip pulse

### 3.1 First-run onboarding (`onboarding-*.png`)

Owner, 2026-11-08: the welcome becomes a proper onboarding. It shows, in game, how the site, the addon, the companion and the apps work as one loop. It replaces both the three chat lines and round 2's chip callout. **Status: approved by the owner (2026-11-08), with two coordinator rulings below (the small QR, and a player hiding their score). It ships only once `foreversixty.gg/app` answers on main (`curl -sI https://foreversixty.gg/app`).**

**When and how often**
- Once per account and tour version: `ForeverSixtyDB.tourSeen` holds `Onboarding.TOUR_VERSION` once the player ends it (2026-11-13: deleting the addon folder keeps SavedVariables, so a reinstall never saw the tour again; a changed tour bumps the version and shows once more to everyone). `welcomedAt` stays the first welcome's time. `SiteStatus.welcomeDue` asks the tour's own rule. `/fs tour` replays it any time, and the Settings page lists the command.
- 5 s after the first `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD` of that login, never in combat (waits for `PLAYER_REGEN_ENABLED`), never inside an instance (waits for the next zone change outside).
- Entering combat while it is open hides it without stamping. It returns after combat once, on the page it was on, then not again that login.
- `/fs help` reopens it at page 1 and prints the command list to chat (`settingsCommands`, unchanged). `/fs welcome` (testers) stays as an alias.

**Frame**
- 640 wide, at most 450 tall, `DIALOG` strata. The panel's chrome (§4.2 Look, revised 2026-11-10): `Widgets.umberFrame`, the title band with the bronze-ring medallion and faction notch, the page's heading in bone in the title font over a muted `Welcome to Forever Sixty`, and `2 of 5` on the right; the client's red close X and red buttons. The loop's arrows are flat brass shapes, never font glyphs.
- Footer: `Skip tour` bottom left on every page. On page 1 only, beside it, `Tooltips only`: it turns on the `Tooltips only` switch (§3.3) and ends the tour, one click for a minimalist. `Back` (hidden on page 1) and `Next` bottom right; the page count is in the title band, so no dots. `Tooltips only` has a hover: its name as the gold title, then `Turns off everything but the tooltip lines.` and `Ends the tour. /fs tour opens it again.` in white.
- Height (revised 2026-11-10): each page is as tall as its content, 240 to 450 (`Onboarding.frameHeight`), anchored by its top so the title band never moves between pages.
- Page 2's `One line on every item` row carries a drawn tooltip on a real item (`surfaces/TourExample.lua`): the character's first upgrade from the panel (name in its quality colour, the green arrow and figure, `<slot> · <source>`), else the Data pick for its spec at its band or the band ahead (`Best in slot` or `Best in slot at level 20`, then the source line). Nothing when neither exists.
- The last page has no `Next` and no `Skip tour`: it shows `Open my panel` (closes the tour and opens the panel) and `Done`.
- Ways to leave:

- **Page 1 loop**; R; the player sees the addon as a set of marks and never learns why the site, the companion and the guild matter.
- **Page 2 source labels**; R; the arrow and the ring look like the addon's opinion, not the site's BiS, sim and guide.
- **Page 3 with the logging step**; R, T; the player installs the companion and gets no reports, because logging was off.
- **Page 3 tooltip**; R; "Forever Score" is an abstract number until they see where others see it.
- **Page 4, guild only, one `coming` line**; R; the player in a guild does not know Discord answers work today, or expects in-game answers that do not exist yet.

- **Address boxes**; T; addons cannot open links, and an address the player must retype from memory is lost.
- **The companion's last-seen line**; R; a raider cannot tell whether the site has their latest fights.
- **The QR**; T; the phone app is the one piece a PC player cannot reach by typing in game.
- **`Just the tooltips` on page 1**; T; a minimalist has to find Settings to quiet the addon.
- **Skip tour on every page, Escape, Enter, Done**; T; a player who knows the addon is trapped in five pages.

### 3.2 The chip pulse (`chip-pulse.png`)

- While `ForeverSixtyDB.chipSeenAt` is unset, a gold ring (ffd100) breathes around the chip after each login: alpha 0.3 to 1.0 and back in 1.6 s, ease in and out, five cycles (8 s), then still. An `AnimationGroup` with a loop count, no `OnUpdate`.
- `chipSeenAt` is stamped by the first click on the chip or the first hover over it; after that the first-run pulse never plays again on the account. If neither happens, the pulse gives up after its third login (`ForeverSixtyDB.chipPulses` counts them).
- Never in combat; it does not play while the chip is hidden (talent window open, right-click hidden).
- The existing new-point ring (a few seconds when a point arrives) keeps working after that.
- The chip itself: full opacity always; never an icon without words. Idle text: the next talent level (`Next talent at 25`), else `Forever Sixty`. An actionable state (`Take`, `Equip`, the raid) always outranks the idle line.
- New chip state, highest priority from 18 hours before a raid's start until 6 hours after it (`RaidAssign.SHOWN_FOR_SECONDS`): `Tonight 8:00 pm: Onyxia's Lair`, from the same inbox data as the panel's Tonight region. Once the panel has been opened that day, the raid drops back to normal priority for the session until one hour before the pull.
- Hover, two dim lines: `Click: open Forever Sixty` and `Right-click: hide until level N` (`hide until /fs chip` at 60). The chip's own line repeats first only when the chip had to shorten it.

## Appendix A: purpose audit

Each surface: its moment and the 3-second answer, then one line per element: **element**; the player (R reads, D decides, T taps or clicks); what breaks without it.

**Item tooltip** (hovering gear anywhere; "is this better for me, and by how much")
- **Verdict line**; R, D (equip, keep, sell, roll); the addon has no answer and the player reads stats by hand.
- **Green arrow on the verdict**; R; the tooltip and the bag mark stop reading as one signal.
- **Support line: source under a next upgrade**; R, D (where to go); the player knows what to want but not where it drops.
- **Support line: `Best in slot until level N`**; R; the player cannot tell a good upgrade from the one to keep for ten levels.
- **Support line: `Better: <pick>`**; D (equip now or hold out); a player equips a small upgrade and stops looking for the real one.
- **Support line: `At level N: <pick>` under a worn pick**; R; the next chase is invisible until the band changes.
- **`over <worn>` on rings and trinkets**; D (which ring to replace); the player swaps out the wrong ring.
- **Shift: breakdown**; R when doubting a number; a sceptic cannot check the figure.
- **Shift: alternatives**; D when the pick is out of reach; the player has no fallback without the site.
- **Shift: attribution**; R; nobody knows whether a number is the shipped estimate or their own sim.
- **Shift: cap, enchant, build-list lines**; R; rare caveats, wrong conclusions without them.
- **Compare tooltip titled Next upgrade**; R; the next upgrade is a name without its real stats (tenet 2).

**Loot roll** (a drop in a group; "do I need this")
- **Green arrow on the icon**; R, D; the Need decision waits for a tooltip.
- **`+5.8 DPS`, `Best in slot`, `Not for you` under the name**; D; the player hovers mid-fight or rolls blind.

**Quest reward** (turning in; "which one do I take")
- **Green arrow on the best reward**; D, T; the player hovers all three and compares by hand.
- **`Best in slot` label**; D; a band pick looks like any other upgrade.
- **`Sells for most`**; D, T; with nothing useful, the player opens three tooltips to find the vendor price.

### 6.1 Item 1: the tour steps aside for the Builds dialog

**The one question:** I pressed Change. What do I pick?
**Visible at once:** the dialog, fully opaque, with nothing behind it that competes.

- Any `BuildDialog.open` while the tour is shown calls `Onboarding.stepAside()`. The hook is in `BuildDialog.open`, not in the `Change` button, so `/fs follow`, `/fs build` and the panel's `Change` behave the same way.
- `stepAside` hides the tour frame without ending it, with the same guard the combat hide uses, so `OnHide` does not stamp `welcomedAt`. It remembers the page.
- When the dialog hides (`BuildDialog.close`, the X, Escape, or a build used), `Onboarding.stepBack()` runs `Onboarding.open` on the remembered page, so `Onboarding.inputs()` is read again. A build picked in the dialog shows as the new `Your path: Retribution build`. A dialog closed with the X or Escape redraws the same page unchanged.
- The dialog is opaque (`Theme.ALPHA.window` is 0.96; the see-through look was the tour drawn over it at the same strata) and `BuildDialog.open` raises it (`SetToplevel`, `Raise`). Nothing shows through it.
- Combat while aside: nothing happens, because the tour is already hidden. If the dialog closes in combat, `Onboarding.open` queues the page (`pendingAfterCombat`) and the tour returns at `PLAYER_REGEN_ENABLED`.
- Escape closes the dialog first, since the tour is hidden; the tour then comes back, and a second Escape ends it for good as today.

### 6.2 Item 2: the spec question

**The one question:** which build is mine?
**Answer visible at once:** on the tour's last page, the panel and the chip card, the question with the spec buttons under it. Once answered, `Your path: Retribution build` and `Change`.

**Reference.** The retail Choose a Specialization frame: a spec name on a button, one line of what it plays like under it. For the answer shown as remembered, the retail talent loadout menu: named rows, the active one marked.

#### The rule (verbatim; one function owns it: `Talents.decideTree`)

`Talents.decideTree(points)` takes the points spent per tree (`Talents.tabPoints`) and returns the tree index or nil, and the trees that hold points in tab order. Pure. Let `total` be the sum over the trees, `leader` the largest, `second` the next largest.
1. Sure when at least `SURE_MIN_POINTS` (2) points are spent and every one of them is in one tree.
2. Sure when the leading tree holds more than half of all spent points (`2 * leader > total`) and leads every other tree by at least `SURE_LEAD` (5) points.
3. Everything else asks: zero points, one point, a tie, a split.

Worked cases: 0 asks. 1 asks. 2 in one tree is sure. 2/2 asks. 5/0 is sure. 8/4 asks (lead 4). 10/3 is sure (10 > 6.5, lead 7). 6/1/1 is sure (6 > 4, lead 5). 10/5/5 asks (half the points sit elsewhere). 31/20 is sure. 20/20/11 asks. 12/12 asks.

#### Where the rule applies

| Consumer | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Default build (`Follow.ensureDefault`) | `Gear.specOf`: most points, ties to the first tree | `Gear.specOf` calls `Talents.decideTree`. Undecided with no answer: loads nothing, `Follow.noBuildReason` is `L.diagBuildUndecided` with the question. |
| `Gear.specOf` | Always answered | Answers nil when undecided. Every caller treats nil as "nothing to score": `Gear.upgrades`, `Gear.lines`, `Progress.weightsFor`, `Rotation.specBands`, `Options.currentSpec`, `Comms.ownSpec`. |
| Comms hello | spec from `Comms.ownSpec` | The same; undecided sends no spec (the wire's existing empty field) |
| Guild tab, own row | Spec column | Empty cell when nil, no dash, no word |
| Inspect record of others | `highestTreeName()` | `decideTree` on the inspected points. Nobody to ask: undecided is nil, and the spec column is empty. The client's own `inspectedSpecName()` still wins when it answers. |
| Feral tree (cat or bear) | `treeSpec` ties to the cat | Unchanged (lane ruling above). |

**Precedence** for the player's own spec (`Follow.currentSpec`, `Follow.ensureDefault`):
1. The followed build's spec (`Follow.buildSpec`).
2. The remembered answer (`pathChosen`, per character, stored by `Chip.choosePath`), which loads that spec's default.
3. A tree `decideTree` is sure of.
4. Nil, which asks.

**Consequence the owner should see.** While the spec is undecided nothing loads. Item tooltips show nothing per spec (§2.2 case 3), the bag arrows and the talent ring are absent, and the rotation row is absent. Under level 10 that is every fresh character until they answer. That is the price of never guessing. The chip reads `Pick a path`, so the way out is always one click.

**The site's own spec derivation** (`Gear.specOf`'s old comment said the site uses the same rule) keeps the old rule until the web lane changes it. Until then a 2/2 paladin can read Holy on the site and be asked in game. Needs the coordinator.

#### The question, exact

- Names: the trees that hold points when two or more do, else all the class's trees. Format `Are you %s?` (`L.pathAsk`), joined by `, ` and a last ` or `.

| Character | Heading |
|---|---|
| Paladin 2 Holy, 2 Retribution | `Are you Holy or Retribution?` |
| Paladin, 0 points | `Are you Holy, Protection or Retribution?` |
| Druid Feral Combat 8, Restoration 6 | `Are you Feral Combat or Restoration?` |
| Druid, 0 points | `Are you Balance, Feral Combat or Restoration?` |

- The buttons are always every spec the data has builds for (`Chip.pathSpecs`), in tree order: paladin 3, druid 4 (Feral and Feral Bear beside each other). A named tree narrows the heading, never the buttons: the player may be none of them.
- Nothing is pre-picked and nothing is required. `Done`, Escape and closing the card all leave it unanswered.

#### Where it shows

- **Tour, last page.** Heading in ink at normal size where `Pick your path` stood. The existing muted note stays beside it (`loads its leveling build`, `loads its raid build` at 60). Tiles are the client's red buttons, spec name on the button, `specPlaystyle` under it. Tile width is `floor((588 - 8 * (n - 1)) / n)` (`Onboarding.tileWidth`): 190 for 3, 141 for 4. At 4 across the playstyle wraps to at most 2 lines.

The card heading is the question in gold, set on every show (the points change while the card is built once); the race-and-class heading goes. The footer reads `Loads the raid build. You can change it later.` at 60.

**How "remembered" shows.** Once answered, the tour's last page reads `Your path: Retribution build`, its count, its next point and `Change`; the panel's Talents row carries the build's name. `Change` opens the dialog, whose heading is the question again with the answered spec's row marked `Following`.
