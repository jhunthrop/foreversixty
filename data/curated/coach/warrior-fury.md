## Who they are

The best Fury warrior on Forever plays two weapons as one rage engine. Bloodthirst goes out the moment it is ready, every time, and everything else in the kit is arranged around that button. They never sit on a full rage bar, never let Battle Shout fall off, and never go into a boss without the flask, food and stone the pull asks for. What sets them apart is not a secret talent or a trick: it is the same short priority, pressed in the same order, for the whole fight.

## What they know

**The priority, in words.** This is the order the site's Fury rotation runs, and the order they play. Bloodrage whenever it is ready and the bar is not near the top, because rage limits everything else Fury does. Death Wish and Recklessness for the execute phase, as the burst window. Battle Shout kept up for the group. In execute range, Execute comes first, ahead of Bloodthirst and Whirlwind. Otherwise Bloodthirst whenever it is off cooldown, since it is the highest damage per rage in the kit. Whirlwind fills the time while Bloodthirst is more than a global cooldown away. Heroic Strike is the rage dump, and only with forty rage or more spare, so it never starves Bloodthirst or Execute.

**Cooldowns and how they line up.** Death Wish is on a three-minute cooldown. When the fight is long enough for a second use after this one, press it now; otherwise hold it for execute range, where Execute takes the extra damage, and never hold it so long that the fight ends before it runs out. Recklessness is a once-a-fight button and goes in execute range too. On a short pull, press both in the closing seconds rather than not at all. Death Wish also raises the damage you take while it runs, which is worth knowing on a boss that hits the raid. Bloodrage is a minute-long cooldown that turns a sliver of health into rage, and it is pressed early enough that the rage lands while Bloodthirst still wants it. Battle Shout goes up just before the pull, so the first swings already carry it.

**What changed for Forever.** Whirlwind now strikes with both weapons. Unbridled Wrath gives its rage with any weapon, not only a two-hander. Bloodthirst scales harder with attack power than it once did, which makes it even more clearly the centre of the rotation. Warriors wear plate from forty. Fury is the dual-wield spec, so both weapons matter, and Dual Wield Specialization is what makes the off hand pull its weight.

**The mistakes seen most, by finding.**
- core_casts: Bloodthirst sitting ready with rage in the bar, because Whirlwind or Heroic Strike went first or the warrior was walking.
- resource: rage at the cap. A full bar is rage thrown away; Heroic Strike is the answer, and Bloodrage pressed on a full bar is a wasted cooldown.
- idle: late to the boss after a move or a phase change, swinging at nothing while the boss walks.
- uptime: Battle Shout lapsing in a long fight, usually after a death or a phase change.
- adherence: buttons outside the list taking globals Bloodthirst and Whirlwind could have used.
- ranks: a rotation spell left at an old rank because the trainer visit was skipped.
- preparation: a missing flask, food or sharpening stone, or a combat potion never used.
- death: dying inside Death Wish, or standing where the boss cleaves.

**Consumables and world buffs.** For a damage dealer the catalogue looks for Flask of the Titans, Grilled Squid, an Elemental Sharpening Stone on the weapons, and the Mighty Rage Potion, up to its use limit in a fight. The world buffs that matter are Rallying Cry of the Dragonslayer, Warchief's Blessing, Songflower Serenade and Spirit of Zandalar. They are listed as facts, never held against anyone, because nobody controls when they drop.

**Ten players against forty.** In a forty-player raid the buffs pile up: several warriors, a shaman or a paladin, every class's raid buff. On a ten-player night there may be no second warrior, so your Battle Shout is the only one the melee gets, and losing it costs more people than you. Fewer healers means a death lands harder too, and Death Wish's extra damage taken is a real cost. A missing flask is a smaller deal on a ten-player boss than on a forty; a missing weapon enchant is the same in both.

## Drills

- drill.core_casts: On a target dummy, press Bloodthirst the moment it comes off cooldown, before anything else; Whirlwind and Heroic Strike wait their turn.
- drill.uptime: Put Battle Shout where you can see its timer. Recast it with 10 seconds left, and recast it the moment you are back up after a death or a phase change.
- drill.idle: On every move, keep the boss in range and swing the moment you arrive. Practise on trash: the first button after a reposition is Bloodthirst, not a walk to the side.
- drill.resource: Watch the rage bar on a dummy. At 40 or more spare with Bloodthirst cooling down, queue Heroic Strike; at the cap, it should already be queued.
- drill.ranks: Visit your trainer every time you level and read your spellbook after: Bloodthirst, Whirlwind, Execute, Heroic Strike and Battle Shout should all show their top rank.
- drill.preparation: Before every boss, check four things: flask, food buff, sharpening stone on both weapons, a Mighty Rage Potion on your bars. Do it on the walk to the pull, not after it.
- drill.death: Before the pull, find the spot behind the boss, out of its cleave. With Death Wish running, keep a healing potion on a key you can reach without looking.
- drill.adherence: On a dummy, play only Bloodrage, Death Wish, Battle Shout, Execute, Bloodthirst, Whirlwind and Heroic Strike for three minutes. Anything else on your bars waits.
