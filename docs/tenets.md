# Tenets

1. **The standard is the best WoW addon ever made.** Every surface we ship, in game and on the site, is judged against Details!, WeakAuras and ElvUI at their best, not against "it works". A plain table where an icon, a quality colour or a tooltip was available is a defect.
2. **An item is never just a name.** Wherever an item appears (BiS lists, planner slots, reports, tooltips, the addon), it shows its icon, its quality colour and, on hover, the real item: stats, item level, required level, source. In game that is the client's own item tooltip; on the site it is the shared item tooltip, the same way talents already work.
3. **An ability is never just a name.** Rotation lines carry the spell's icon, rank and the condition in plain words.
4. **Nothing is clipped, off-frame or hidden behind a disclosure when it is the point.** A runner-up, a swap note or a "load it" button is shown, not tucked away.
5. **Accuracy first, then polish, and both before ship.** A wrong number with a beautiful icon is still wrong; a right number in a plain list is still unshipped.
6. **Design before build, and two reviewers see it before you do.** A player-facing surface starts from an experience spec and mock written by the `ux-designer` agent and reviewed by the `wow-player` persona; the lane builds to the spec. Before merge, both review the rendered result on screenshots at phone and desktop with every state, never a diff. A green test suite is not "done"; two SHIP verdicts on screenshots are.
7. **Data reads like the game, not like the pipeline.** A source is one line a player recognises (boss and instance, quest and giver, vendor and faction, "World drop"), never a dump of every mob. A weight carries its unit and its DPS meaning. A pick shows its alternatives. Pipeline counters and coverage numbers stay out of the player's view.
8. **Everything we ship is verified 100% accurate before it ships.** Every pick, stat, source, drop chance, weight, level and rotation line is checked against a primary source (the client's own tables, a sim run, classic-db, the game itself) and the check is recorded in the lane report. Nothing we cannot verify is shown as fact: it is labelled as unverified or left out. Known-good fixtures pin what a theorycrafter would call obvious, and a change that moves them fails the build until a person explains why the new answer is right.
9. **Every interactive element has every state, and each is verified in a browser.** Default, hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, loading, empty and error; keyboard and touch parity. A promise the site makes (hover shows the item) is exercised by an end-to-end test on every page that makes it.
10. **One design system, no invention.** Tokens, the type scale, spacing, quality colours and the shared controls (pill, tooltip, tab strip, table row, card, disclosure) are the only building blocks. A lane never invents a control; a new shared component is its own design review.
11. **Hierarchy over density.** One primary element per region, one level of emphasis, deliberate whitespace. A wall of equal-weight rows is a defect even when every row has an icon.
12. **Compare to the reference before calling it done.** Side by side with the best-in-class for that surface (the in-game panel, Wowhead, Details!, ElvUI), and the report says why ours is as good or better. If the author cannot say it, it is not done.
13. **Polish is measurable and part of done.** Lighthouse budgets, no layout shift on hydration, no missing-icon flash, no font swap. They are not a gate to relax in order to ship.
14. **We help a player play their class better; we are not a reference site.** Every surface leads with the player's class or character and the next thing that makes it better (the BiS gap, the talent build, the sim, the log), never with a slogan or a feature list. The front door is "pick your class" or "your character", and the line above it is "Play your class better." Reference facts (dates, sources, changelog) stay, one level down, in service of that.
