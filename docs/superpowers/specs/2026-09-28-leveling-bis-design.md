# Leveling BiS: the best gear at every level band, from the simulator

**Date:** 2026-09-28. **Owner ask:** "use the simulator and top gear to show what is the BiS at each level range as the person levels ... at level 20 a BiS for all equippable level 20 gear, with faction isolation for quests, etc. ... as they progress in level, it shows the new items that are BiS."

## Yes, and with what exists

- **Candidates:** `data/builds/<active>/items.json` carries every item's required level, class mask, inventory type, quality and faction restriction (23,909 items incl. the wowhead supplement).
- **Sources:** the engine's item database (`assets/database/db.json`, wowsims-forever) names, per item, its quest (id and name; the item's faction restriction carries the quest's side: 336 Alliance, 332 Horde, 560 both among 1,228 quest items), its vendor, its dungeon or raid boss, its crafting profession, its reputation standing, and for 3,292 items the creature and zone it drops from. The site's `loot.json` flattens quests into one bucket today; it grows.
- **Scoring:** the simulator's stat-weights mode (`sim/request/weights.go`) run for the spec at the band's level on the accuracy program's ladder character gives the band's weights; an item's score is the weighted sum of its stats (the planner already computes item stats per level through curves). Instant to rank thousands of candidates.
- **Verification:** Top Gear (`sim/bulk`, mode gear) sims the ranked set at that level to confirm the DPS and to settle the close calls per slot.

## Built once per spec and band, never per character

The owner (2026-09-28): "the BiS lists should be built once per class and level. It shouldn't be character by character." The ranking and the Top Gear verification run once per written spec per band, nightly, and the result is published as data. Per spec rather than per class because the stat weights that rank the items differ by spec (Balance and Feral want opposite gear); a class with one damage spec on the site is one list. A signed-in player's "your character" view is a lookup against that published list -- which of the saved export's slots the list beats -- and never a simulation of that character.

## Definitions

- **Bands:** 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60. A band's list is "the best you can wear at level L".
- **Eligible at L:** required level <= L; class allowed; armor proficiency by level (mail for hunters and shamans from 40, plate for warriors and paladins from 40; before that the lower class); weapon skills the class can train; faction: the item's restriction and the quest's side must match the chosen faction; raid items excluded below 60 (a leveling list is about what a leveling character can get); PvP rank items shown only with their rank; crafted items shown with the profession as a note (a "with professions" toggle); reputation items with the standing.
- **Obtainable, not lucky:** a slot's BiS is the best item with a named source (quest, dungeon, vendor, crafted, rep). World drops with a known zone are listed separately as "if it drops" candidates, never as the answer, because a leveling player cannot farm a 0.1% green.
- **New at this band:** the diff against the previous band per slot: the item, its source, the DPS gain the verification sim measured for the whole set.

## The pipeline

1. **Data (`data/pipeline/loot`):** enrich `loot.json` from db.json: quest sources become `{quest_id, name, faction}` per item; add `vendor` and `zone-drop` kinds (npc, zone name, zone level range from `zones`). Keep the existing kinds. Tests pin the counts.
2. **Ranking (`sim/cmd/leveling-bis`, Go):** for each written spec and band: build the ladder character (talents truncated; the same rule the ladder test states), run the weights mode once, score every eligible candidate per slot, pick greedily with the two-slot and one-hand/two-hand rules `sim/bulk/expand.go` already encodes, then run one Top Gear pass of the chosen set plus the runner-up per slot to confirm. Emit `data/builds/<active>/leveling-bis/<spec>.json`: per band, per slot, the item, its source, the score, the verified set DPS, and the diff from the previous band.
3. **Schedule:** nightly beside the sim validation job (Cloud Run `sim-run`/`sim-validate` recipes in `api/README.md`); the output is data, committed by the pipeline like loot.json, and published under `/data/<build>/`.
4. **Site:** a "Leveling gear" section per spec guide and a page `/gear/<class>/<spec>?level=20&faction=horde`: a band slider, per-slot rows with source badges (quest name and zone, boss and dungeon, vendor, profession, rep), a "New at 25" strip, an Alliance/Horde toggle, and for a signed-in player a "your character" mode that marks which slots are upgrades over the saved export and links each to the simulator's Top Gear with the candidate preloaded.
5. **Addon (design pass wave C):** Data.lua carries, per class/spec/band, the BiS item ids; the tooltip says "BiS for level 20-24 Marksmanship"; the level-up toast lists the new BiS items with their source.

## What is honest about it

The list is only as good as the sources: an item db.json has no source for is invisible, so the page says "N items had no known source" per band. Faction isolation covers quest side and item restriction; neutral quests show for both. Weights-based ranking can misorder two close items; the Top Gear pass settles the top two per slot but not every pair, and the page marks the verified rows.

## Guardrails (lane `rank-guardrails`, 2026-09-29)

The checks above (`sim/cmd/leveling-bis/published_test.go`'s `TestPublishedBISFilesPassSanityChecks`) confirm the published picks are internally consistent, but by themselves they can only re-derive facts about what the pipeline *did* publish - they have no opinion on what a slot *should* have shown, or on how much of the game's own gear the ranker could even see in the first place. Two guardrails close that gap:

- **Coverage.** For every band and slot, `buildBandPool` (`band.go`) now counts how many items `eligible()` passed for that band+faction (level, class, faction - the same gate every pick already goes through) and how many of those `sourceFor()` could actually find a usable source for. This is published on every band as `coverage: { "<slot>": { "eligible": N, "sourced": M } }` (`report.go`'s `bandReport.Coverage`, `slotRow`'s own sibling field) - a slot missing from the map had zero eligible candidates at all, not even an unsourced one. The nightly log prints one line per band+faction naming the band-wide total and the single worst-covered slot (`report.go`'s `coverageSummary`), so a reader does not have to open the JSON to see how much of the "N items had no known source" figure above is concentrated in one slot. The web read contract (`web/src/lib/bis/types.ts`'s `BisBand.coverage`, normalised to `{}` for an older file by `load.ts`'s `normaliseBisFile`) exists so the page (a later lane) can show this per slot rather than only as the band-wide `no_source_count`.
- **Known-good sanity.** `sim/cmd/leveling-bis/testdata/known-good.json` is a small, hand-curated list of items the Classic community agrees are best or near-best for six specs (hunter-marksmanship, rogue-combat, warrior-arms, mage-frost, warlock-affliction, priest-shadow) at bands 20, 40 and 60, every id checked against the real item file before being committed. `TestKnownGoodItemsAreSeenAndRanked` (`knowngood_test.go`, gated by `FOREVER_BIS_SANITY=1`, the same nightly warning step as the sanity check above) asks the harder question per item: is it eligible, is it sourced, and was it actually competitive (picked, the runner-up, or scoring at least as well as the published pick)? It fails on exactly two things: an eligible item with no source (invisible to the list no matter how good it is), or an item that out-scores the published pick yet was neither picked nor the runner-up (a real ranking gap - a pairing/two-hand/cross-class-set exclusion, or a `pick()` bug - not just a close call against a legitimately better item). The first run against the committed `data/builds/1.60.1.70009/bis/*.json` found 19 well-known items across all six specs invisible for exactly this reason - see the lane report for the list.

## What the sim counts

2026-09-28, lane `weights-effects`. The owner, looking at a level-20 hunter's stat weights: "Do we factor in on-hit effects, set bonuses, etc. in our sims?" Honest answer, in numbers, as of this build (`1.60.1.70009`) and this fork pin plus this lane's own three new item effects:

| | items with an effect (proc/on-use/on-hit) | engine implements | sets worn by an item | engine implements the set |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Whole build** (every class's `items/*.json`, deduped by id) | 386 | 40 | 295 | 109 |
| **`data/builds/<build>/sets.json`** (every set this build knows, whether or not an item wears it yet) | -- | -- | 536 | 147 |
| **Tonight's committed BiS picks** (`data/builds/<build>/bis/*.json`, every band/faction/spec) | 20 of 373 distinct items | 6 | 26 | 20 |
| **Top-3-scored candidates per slot, bands 10-50** (every written spec) | 18 of 485 distinct candidates | 4 | -- | -- |

Methodology, so a reader can re-run it: "engine implements" an item's effect means some file under the fork's `sim/` registers `core.NewItemEffect` (or an `itemhelpers.CreateWeapon*Proc*` helper, which calls it) with that item's numeric id as the registration's own item argument, in a file Go actually compiles (a file named with a leading underscore, e.g. `sim/priest/_items.go`, is excluded -- Go does not build it, so priest currently has zero implemented item effects however many that file claims). "Engine implements the set" means a `core.NewItemSet(core.ItemSet{Name: "..."})` call somewhere in `sim/*/item_sets_pve.go`/`item_sets_pvp.go` (rogue: `items_sets_pve.go`/`items_sets_pvp.go`) or `sim/common/item_sets/*.go` has a `Name` matching `sets.json`'s own name for that set id. Both are a two-pass source scrape, not a parse of Go's own call graph -- see `sim/cmd/leveling-bis/effectids_generated.go` and `setids_generated.go`'s own doc comments for the exact regeneration procedure, which this lane's report also gives verbatim. A "registered" set can still be a partial implementation: `sim/hunter/item_sets_pve.go`'s own Giantstalker 3-piece bonus is a `// Not implemented in sim` stub that still counts as "registered" here, because this table answers "does the engine run SOME function for this set", not "is every one of its bonuses real" -- the finer distinction is what an actual verified sim (`sim/cmd/leveling-bis/sets.go`'s `trySetCompletion`) settles per band, not this count.

The 40-of-386 whole-build figure undercounts what the engine can DO versus what THIS SITE's own data marks as having an effect: 194 item ids have some registered effect fork-wide, but only 40 of them fall inside the 386 ids this build's `items/*.json` gives a non-empty `effect_text` -- the rest are set-piece bonuses, enchant effects, or items outside a class's leveling item pool entirely (raid-only trinkets long since implemented for level 60 progression, not for this table's own "does a leveling candidate's proc get simulated" question).

**Before this lane, the ranking could not see any of this.** `sim/cmd/leveling-bis/score.go`'s `score()` is a weighted sum of an item's flat `Stats` plus its weapon DPS -- a proc or a set bonus contributes exactly nothing to it, the same "dishonest tie-break" gap the prototype's own trinket-ranking code (`trinkets.go`) already called out for trinkets specifically, generalized nowhere else. This lane's `rank.go`/`sets.go` close that for every slot: a candidate whose effect the engine implements gets a real engine-verified DPS comparison against the slot's score()-based pick (the same shape `trinkets.go` already gives trinkets); a pick that would complete an engine-implemented 2- or 3-piece set is tried together and kept only if it verifies ahead of the independently-scored picks; and a pick whose effect the engine does NOT implement is still scored on stats alone but the report row now carries `effect_unmodelled: true`, so the page can say "proc not simulated" instead of implying the number accounts for everything the item does.

**What this lane's fork work closed, and what is left.** Three of the ten highest-value gaps this table's own "top-3 candidates, bands 10-50" column surfaced now have real engine effects (`sim/common/item_effects/leveling_procs.go` on the fork): Frost Tiger Blade's frost bolt, Blight's disease (instant + DoT), and Dark Iron Rifle's Shadow Shot. The other seven this lane identified but did not implement, with the mechanism each needs: Helm of Fire (on-use nuke, `core.NewItemEffect` + activated spell, the "Kiss of the Spider" shape), Barbaric Belt (on-use rage grant), Bleakwood Hew (a stacking magic-resistance-reduction debuff on hit, needs a target-side aura, not just damage), Girdle of Reprisal (a reactive proc on being struck, `CallbackOnSpellHitTaken` rather than `...Dealt`), Dark Iron Pulverizer (an on-use stun, CC/diminishing-returns-adjacent), and two "weaken this specific questgiver NPC" quest-interaction effects (Enchanted Azsharite Felbane Staff/Dagger) that are arguably not worth engine-modeling at all -- they trigger against one quest NPC, not in combat DPS terms. Skull of Impending Doom, the single highest-reach candidate in the top-3 table (16 of 20 written specs' pools), was deliberately left out: its effect is a self-damaging movement/escape cooldown, never a DPS button, so simulating it would not change a single ranking -- it is correctly ranked on its plain Intellect stat today, and `effect_unmodelled: true` on it is the honest, low-cost answer rather than engineering effort a DPS sim gets no value from.
